1. Reporting a vulnerability

Email support@7thcode.app with the subject Security report: [product name]. Include the affected product and version, potential impact, safe reproduction steps, and whether any customer data may be involved. Do not create a public issue or include credentials, access tokens, account IDs, confidential Jira content, or live customer data. We will arrange a safer method if sensitive evidence is necessary.

2. Testing boundaries

Use only accounts, sites, projects, and data you own or are expressly authorized to test. Keep testing proportionate and minimize access, modification, and retention. Stop immediately and report privately if you encounter another person’s data or gain unintended access.

Do not perform denial-of-service or load testing, social engineering, phishing, malware delivery, physical attacks, credential attacks, destructive testing, privacy violations, data exfiltration, or testing against a customer production tenant without written authorization. Do not publicly disclose a suspected issue before the Provider has had a reasonable opportunity to investigate and remediate it. These guidelines do not authorize conduct that is unlawful or prohibited by Atlassian or another applicable agreement.

3. Common security baseline

  • Use the minimum app permissions and data necessary for documented functionality.
  • Avoid external data egress where a product can operate fully within Atlassian Forge, and disclose product-specific hosting and remotes.
  • Keep credentials and confidential customer content out of operational logs and ordinary support messages.
  • Use dependency pinning, automated verification, code review, and release checks proportionate to the product and its risk.
  • Investigate credible reports and provide security fixes or mitigations according to severity, feasibility, and applicable Marketplace obligations.

Only the latest version available through the Atlassian Marketplace is supported unless product documentation expressly says otherwise.

CloneGuard uses approval-bound contracts, user-context permission checks, serialized background work, idempotency markers, artifact journals, and postflight verification to reduce unauthorized or duplicate Jira writes. It has no Jira delete path. These controls reduce risk but do not guarantee that every defect, platform failure, or unsafe result is impossible.

For authoritative product detail, see Flow Guard security reporting, Flow Guard privacy, and StatusLens security. Product documentation controls where it is more specific.

5. Response and remediation

Reports are reviewed in Japanese on a reasonable-efforts basis during Japan business days, 10:00–17:00 JST. We may acknowledge, request more information, reproduce, assess severity, prepare a fix or mitigation, and coordinate disclosure. Security and data-integrity risks are prioritized when reasonably possible, but no acknowledgement, remediation, release, recovery, or disclosure timeline is guaranteed. Product-specific documentation may state a non-binding target.

There is no monetary bug-bounty or reward program unless agreed in writing before testing. Submission of a report does not create a contract, employment relationship, entitlement to payment, or obligation to publish credit.

6. Security limitations

No software, hosting platform, transmission, or storage method is completely secure. This policy is not a certification, audit report, warranty, insurance commitment, safe-harbor promise, or representation of Atlassian Marketplace Bug Bounty, Cloud Fortified, or any other program unless a product’s current listing expressly says so. Service use remains subject to the Product Terms.
